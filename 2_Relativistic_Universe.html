Special Relativity Reference

1. Relativistic Aberration

As an observer moves at relativistic velocities, the apparent angles of photons from stars shift forward in the direction of motion. The stars are physically stationary in their celestial rest frame; only the incoming photon vectors transform under the Lorentz boost.

cos θ′ = (cos θ + β) / (1 + β cos θ)

• θ = angle from flight vector in star rest frame
• θ′ = apparent angle in observer frame
• Half of the entire sky is contracted into θ′_1/2 = arccos(β)

2. Relativistic Doppler Shift

Photons received from the forward direction are blueshifted to higher frequencies and shorter wavelengths. Photons from the rear are redshifted to longer wavelengths.

D = 1 / [ γ (1 − β cos θ) ]
λ′_obs = λ₀ / D   |   f′_obs = f₀ × D

• Forward (θ = 0): D_fwd = √((1+β)/(1−β)) > 1 (blueshift)
• Rear (θ = π): D_rear = √((1−β)/(1+β)) < 1 (redshift)

3. Relativistic Beaming (Searchlight)

The radiant flux received by the observer transforms proportionally to D³ (or D⁴ for bolometric integration). Forward stars brighten dramatically, while rear stars dim into the blackness of the void.

I_obs ≈ I₀ × D³

4. Lorentz Factor γ

γ = 1 / √(1 − β²)

Governs temporal dilation and spatial contraction. As β → 1, γ → ∞.

5. Physical vs Apparent Reality

The celestial bodies themselves do not physically migrate or converge. What changes is the geometry of light cones intercepted by an observer whose spacetime coordinates are Lorentz-boosted relative to the cosmic frame.
